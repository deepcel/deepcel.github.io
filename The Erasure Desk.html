The Erasure Desk

Imagine the 2026 exposure draft has passed exactly as written. You are the privacy officer at Chirp, a social media service. Under the new APP 14 it counts as a large digital platform, so it must destroy a person's information when they ask, unless one of six refusal grounds applies. Nine requests are in the tray. Stamp each one Erase, Refuse (on one ground) or Escalate if the law genuinely doesn't say.

Chirp at a glance

Service
Social media service (Online Safety Act definition)
Users
6.1 million average Australian end-users
Revenue
$1.4 billion group revenue
Status
Large digital platform: APP 14 applies

Under the draft, the erasure right reaches only large platforms: those with at least $500 million in group revenue and/or 2.5 million average Australian end-users. Chirp clears both bars.

    How to play

    Read the letter, then the internal file note. Facts in the note usually decide the case. A refusal needs the right ground, not just the right stamp. Save Escalate for the one question the draft leaves open.

    Chirp and every requester are invented. The rules come from the Attorney-General's August 2026 exposure draft, the Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026, as summarised in the Module 3 notes. The Bill may change before it passes. This is a learning game, not legal advice.
